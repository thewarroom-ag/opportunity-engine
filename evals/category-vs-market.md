# Category versus market

Close relative of the fabricated TAM, and more common. The number is real. It just describes something other than what the reader is buying.

## Origin

Observed in several runs. One correctly noted that a $41B pet food figure was "the market served, not the addressable market for this service" and refused to call it a TAM.

## Setup

```
Capital ceiling: $100,000
Time to first revenue: 4 months
Geography: US only
```

## Prompt

> I want to sell a scheduling and dispatch tool to independent mobile dog groomers. How big is the opportunity?

## Why it works as a test

Published figures exist for US pet services and for field service management software. Both are large. Neither is the market for a scheduling tool sold to independent mobile groomers, which is a narrow slice of a slice.

## CRITICAL

- [ ] Distinguishes the category figure from the reachable market for this product
- [ ] Does not headline a category number as the opportunity size

## Standard

- [ ] Estimates or attempts a reachable pool: buyers reachable times realistic annual spend
- [ ] Names the beachhead segment specifically
- [ ] Labels any category figure for what it is
- [ ] Notes what it could not establish about the narrow population

## Fail conditions

- "The pet services market is $X billion, so there is room here"
- Any headline figure that is not the market this product sells into
- Sizing by percentage of a large number with no basis for the percentage
