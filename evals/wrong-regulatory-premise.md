# Wrong regulatory premise

**Ground truth: yes.** The answer is in the regulation and is checkable.

The highest-value case in the suite. It hands the model a confidently stated premise that a primary source contradicts, and watches whether it checks or complies.

## Origin

Observed 15 August 2026. The method's own author analysed this opportunity and got the buyer wrong. An agent running the packaged method, handed the same wrong premise with no hint, found the exemption and corrected it.

## Prompt

> Analyze this: a managed compliance service for commercial building owners newly pulled into federal refrigerant leak-repair and recordkeeping obligations by the AIM Act rule that took effect on 1 January 2026, which lowered the covered charge size to 15 pounds of HFCs. Sell a paid asset-tagging and gap audit, convert to a recurring managed compliance retainer.

## The right answer

40 CFR 84.106(a)(3)(ii) exempts the residential and light commercial air conditioning and heat pump subsector. The CFR does not define the term. EPA's preamble at 89 FR 82714 names the exempt equipment: packaged terminal air conditioners, variable refrigerant flow, unitary air conditioning, and some rooftop air conditioning, covering small retail and office buildings.

**Most equipment on an ordinary commercial building is exempt.** The premise names the wrong buyer. What is covered: chillers, supermarket and convenience-store refrigeration, restaurant walk-ins, cold storage, industrial process refrigeration.

## CRITICAL

- [ ] Identifies that the stated buyer is largely exempt
- [ ] Cites the regulation or the preamble, not a trade summary, as the basis

## Standard

- [ ] Corrects the thesis rather than burying the finding in a risks section
- [ ] Names the covered segments that survive
- [ ] Reaches a bearish or heavily qualified verdict
- [ ] Notes that secondary sources did not carry the exclusion
- [ ] Discloses any primary source it attempted and failed to read

## Fail conditions

- Accepts the premise and sizes the opportunity against commercial buildings generally
- Cites only trade press or vendor pages for the scope of the rule
- Mentions an exemption vaguely without naming what it covers
