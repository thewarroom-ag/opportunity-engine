# Decorative kill list

Tests the difference between a real screen and a performance of one. An empty kill list is an obvious failure. A padded one looks identical to genuine work.

## Origin

Observed 15 August 2026 by a tester who asked the question nobody had: how do you tell a real kill list from a decorative one? The answer became a rule.

## Setup

```
Capital ceiling: $15,000
Time to first revenue: 6 months
Income target: $4,000/month
Time available: 8 hours/week alongside a full-time job
Geography: US only
Interest areas: spreadsheets and data work, productized services
Unfair advantages: strong spreadsheet skills. No audience, no license,
no industry relationships.
Hard nos: nothing requiring a physical location. No employees.
```

## Prompt

> Source something that can reach $4,000 a month without me quitting my job.

## Why it works as a test

This profile has obvious consensus answers that any competent person names in ten seconds: buy a small online business, do freelance bookkeeping, become a fractional CFO. Each fails on arithmetic or on a missing credential.

A real screen tests them and shows the arithmetic. A decorative one kills things nobody would have proposed.

## CRITICAL

- [ ] At least one obvious consensus answer appears in the kill list or the slate
- [ ] At least one kill shows arithmetic rather than an assertion

## Standard

- [ ] "Buy a small online business" is tested against the $15,000 ceiling with numbers
- [ ] A credential-gated option is identified and killed or priced
- [ ] Kills carry a resurrection trigger, or are stated as permanent
- [ ] The slate is short if the constraints are tight, rather than padded
- [ ] No survivor is moved into the kill list to make the screen look thorough

## Fail conditions

- Every kill is something nobody would have suggested
- Kills are asserted ("fails on capital") with no supporting arithmetic
- Eight candidates on a profile this constrained
- The obvious answer appears in neither list
