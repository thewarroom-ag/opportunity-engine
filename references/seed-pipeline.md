# Seed pipeline

A starting universe, for when the user has no category.

**This is a prompt, not a list of answers.** Nothing here is vetted. Every entry still goes through the seven lenses, the extraction sentence, and the screen. Treat these as places to point the lenses.

**The list is incomplete, and it will stay incomplete.** If the Builder Profile points somewhere this file does not cover, hunt there anyway and say the category was not on the list. Do not force a profile into three ill-fitting categories because those are the headings available. The profile beats this file every time.

---

## How to use it

1. Read the Builder Profile.
2. Pick 2 or 3 categories that fit the profile's interests and capital ceiling.
3. Say why you picked them.
4. Run the seven lenses inside those categories.

Do not source from this list directly. A category is not a candidate.

---

## Categories

### B2B SaaS
Vertical software for trades and clinics. Compliance and regulatory tools. Niche professional tools (architecture, law firm intake, insurance agency, recruiting). Horizontal SMB tools: scheduling, inventory, reviews, local search, appointment reminders.

### B2B services
Fractional executives (finance, operations, marketing, technology). Compliance and audit preparation. Technical writing and documentation. Security testing. Data cleanup and migration. Post-acquisition integration.

### Productized services
Fixed-scope, fixed-price, repeatable. Design subscriptions. Bookkeeping. Content operations. Video and podcast production. Lead research. Anything currently sold by the hour that could be sold by the outcome.

### Events and hospitality
Corporate offsites and retreat production. Membership clubs and roaming member formats with no permanent venue. Group travel led by a host or community. Event operations run as a retainer for other people's audiences. Sponsorship packaging for private rooms. Venue sourcing and site selection. Event staffing and crew supply. Conference and trade show services.

### Creator economy
Tools for people who publish. Sponsorship operations. Rights and licensing. Audience data. Course and community infrastructure. Back office for one-person media businesses.

### Marketplaces
Two-sided, where one side is badly served. Expert and advisory networks. Equipment rental. Specialty labor. Surplus and secondary inventory. Local services with trust problems.

### Local and SMB
Route-based services. Home and commercial services. Specialty cleaning. Equipment maintenance. Anything where the incumbent has an aging owner and no software.

### Health and wellness
Clinic operations. Specialty supplements. Recovery and longevity services. Corporate wellness. Practitioner tooling. Note: regulation is heavy here. Check the licensing gate early.

### Education and training
Certification delivery. Compliance training with a legal trigger. Trade skills. Corporate onboarding. Note: check whether an association already gives it away free.

### Fintech adjacent
Not banking. Around it. Invoice and receivables. Spend controls for specific verticals. Insurance operations. Payment reconciliation for messy industries. Tax and entity compliance.

### E-commerce and DTC
Category with a real product gap, not another brand. Supply-constrained goods. Subscription where consumption is genuinely repeated. Private label into an underserved specification.

### Real estate adjacent
Property operations. Small landlord tooling. Commercial building compliance. Facilities and maintenance coordination. Transaction back office.

### Content and media
Trade publications with no modern owner. Newsletters for high-value niches. Data products built on public records. Research subscriptions.

### AI-native
Work that was too expensive to do manually and just became cheap. Document extraction at scale. Monitoring and exception handling. Translation and localization for regulated content. Note: check saturation hard. Most obvious AI applications now have funded competitors.

### Arbitrage
Same service, very different prices by geography, channel, or buyer sophistication. Offshore delivery into premium markets. Wholesale-to-retail gaps. Regulatory arbitrage between states.

## Where the good ones usually are

From the lenses, ranked by hit rate at low capital:

1. **Regulatory forcing.** A dated deadline creates demand you do not have to manufacture. Narrow markets, but real ones.
2. **Structural drift.** Ownership transitions and demographic shifts. Slow, huge, ignored.
3. **Incumbent decay.** Categories where the best-known vendor is disliked and expensive.
4. **Complaint mining.** People already paying, and unhappy. Demand is proven.
5. **Capital flow.** Follows belief, but you arrive after the money. Often crowded.
6. **Cost-curve collapse.** Real, but the obvious plays get funded within a year.
7. **Arbitrage.** Works, but usually needs an unfair advantage in the Builder Profile.

**Warning on capital flow and cost-curve collapse.** These two lenses produce the most exciting-sounding candidates and the most crowded ones. When a lens fires loudly, check saturation before you get attached.

## Categories to approach carefully

| Category | Why |
|---|---|
| Anything with a physical location | Fails the capital gate fast |
| Consumer subscription | High churn, expensive acquisition, no compounding |
| Marketplaces from zero | Cold-start problem is usually beyond six months |
| Government-sold anything | Sales cycles exceed the timeline gate |
| Obvious AI wrappers | Funded competitors, and the model providers ship your feature |

None of these are banned. They just need a stronger reason than usual.
